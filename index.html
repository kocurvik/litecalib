Quick Start

pip install litecalib
import litecalib

model = litecalib.load("litecalib_vitl")         # or litecalib_vitb / litecalib_vits
calib = model.calibrate("photo.jpg", cam_id="radial:1")
calib.K, calib.distortion                       # intrinsics in pixels of the original image
undistorted = calib.undistort("photo.jpg")

Abstract

Recent learning-based methods for single-image camera calibration rely on predicting dense per-pixel fields as an intermediate representation, which can be used to fit the parameters of a specific model. In this paper we show that predicting only a sparse intermediate representation is sufficient and makes it possible to design a significantly faster method while retaining accuracy. Beyond focusing on speed we scale up the training data by utilizing existing datasets as well as collecting a new CommonsPano dataset of 9 891 publicly available panoramas with permissive licenses. We perform an extensive evaluation in terms of direct camera calibration accuracy and performance in downstream 3D vision tasks such as relative pose estimation, SfM, and 3D reconstruction. The evaluation shows that our method is both significantly faster and more accurate compared to the previous state of the art.

Downstream Tasks in 3D

Monocular Depth Estimation

Monocular geometry models expect little lens distortion, so a fisheye photo comes out bent. Undistorting it with LiteCalib's camera first straightens the room.

3D Reconstruction

Feed-forward reconstruction models expect undistorted photos too. LiteCalib calibrates each camera from its photos among the ten, and undistorting them with it brings back the scene and the camera poses.
